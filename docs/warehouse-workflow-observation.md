# Warehouse workflow observation for AUT-346

## Scope and evidence

This record captures the current Laundrorama used-equipment warehouse workflow to guide Production ticket design. The owner confirmed on September 24, 2026 that the latest transcript is sufficient to close the AUT-346 observation task. The principal source is the [September 21 joint warehouse walkthrough](../source-materials/transcripts/2026-09-21-transcript.docx), supported by the [September 16 process discussion](../source-materials/transcripts/2026-09-16-transcript.docx), the [architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md), and the photographed [washer](../source-materials/operations/checklists/washer-checklist.jpg) and [dryer](../source-materials/operations/checklists/dryer-checklist.jpg) checklists. The walkthrough was not a set of separate, formal interviews with every worker. Transcript wording is sometimes imperfect; this record retains only points that are clear from the discussion.

## Current flow and handoffs

1. **Unload and intake.** A technician photographs Machines as they come off an inbound truck, including nameplates, and sends the photos to William. The September 21 walkthrough describes batch capture at the Load level, so recording each Machine should not require the worker to enter all plate facts manually. Inventory identity and Load association are the intake handoff to later work. (September 21, 01:53–03:11, 36:27–36:44.)
2. **Preliminary inspection and disposition.** William demonstrated spinning a washer drum by hand before choosing a Machine for full testing. Audible bearing trouble can make refurbishment uneconomic and lead to using the Machine for parts. This is an early economic screen, not proof that every Machine is operational. Dryer bearing issues were described as more repairable, so the washer example must not become a universal automatic scrap rule. (September 21, 34:13–34:56, 53:13–54:41; September 16, 44:15–46:15.)
3. **Choose work from a queue.** Today William directs the workers. An active order for an untested Machine takes precedence. Without urgent orders, he tends to work from higher-value equipment toward lower-value equipment; “biggest/newest” is a current shorthand when explicit prices are unavailable. James mainly handles dryers and Blake mainly handles washers, though William said people currently do a mix of work. (September 21, 35:16–37:20, 46:10–47:15.)
4. **Test and record evidence.** A worker moves a selected Machine to testing, uses the relevant washer or dryer method, and checks its functions. Machine-specific instructions and basic checklists are available, but the walkthrough did not establish how consistently they are used. Workers currently record functioning equipment on a phone, starting with the serial/nameplate so William can later find the video and tie it to that Machine. A successful functional demonstration is recorded when the Machine works, rather than while it is known to need repair. The current facility does not support the proposed multi-bay, full-cycle recording arrangement. (September 21, 37:38–40:09, 43:36–46:10.)
5. **Diagnose, repair, and wait for parts.** When a fault is not immediately apparent, workers generally bring William in to inspect and direct next steps. Quick checks may happen at the test position; harder work is set aside so active orders keep moving. Parts may come from stock or donor Machines; some conversion parts are ordered ahead of incoming equipment. Current parts are not fully inventoried, and the walkthrough showed uncertain condition for some used boards. A Machine needing more investigation may go back into a mixed stack with a yellow sticker as a reminder. (September 21, 48:10–56:21; September 16, 35:38–38:05.)
6. **Clean and finish.** Once testing shows the Machine works, it normally moves to Veronica for cleaning and retail presentation. The cleaner removes non-original stickers, handles surface condition, and may reveal cosmetic or missing-item work that still needs a decision. William currently looks over outgoing Machines and calls out remaining fixes; the walkthrough did not establish a separate, consistently recorded QA release. Cleaning time varies by Machine. (September 21, 56:21–01:00:23; September 16, 38:05–39:15.)
7. **Store and prepare shipment.** Finished equipment may be returned to a stack until needed. After testing and cleaning, outbound Machines are put on pallets and strapped. Protection depends on transport: shared freight has more handling and generally needs more protection than a dedicated truck. Staff verify the correct Machines; bills of lading and shipping instructions accompany the shipment. William proposed a final condition video before loading, which the walkthrough did not establish as current routine practice. Pallet fit is judged by actual dimensions, not capacity labels alone. (September 21, 55:05–55:54, 01:02:02–01:08:41.)

## Roles, queues, and parallel work

William currently makes many priority, troubleshooting, economic-repair, and outbound quality decisions. Technicians perform intake photography and testing; the current washer/dryer split is a practical assignment, not an exclusive role rule. Veronica handles the cleaning handoff. The walkthrough did not establish a fixed person for every packing or loading action. The target product's Warehouse, Technician, Cleaner, and Owner Admin roles are described in [ARCHITECTURE.md](../ARCHITECTURE.md); they should not be mistaken for fully observed present-day assignments.

The present queues are largely directed by William and physical reminders. Work can proceed in parallel across Machines: sales and listings may start after intake and an acceptable preliminary check while testing, repair, and cleaning are pending; sold Machines then jump ahead for completion. A Machine may need parts while other Machines continue through testing. Cleaning normally follows a working test, though an early clean of one Machine may supply representative listing photos. The proposed future workflow includes explicit worker queues, separate tester and repair tiers, several test bays per technician, centrally tracked parts requests and orders, and audited model-specific instructions. Those were aspirations in the discussion, not current practice. (September 16, 43:30–46:55; September 21, 35:16–36:17, 43:59–47:15, 55:54–56:54, 01:28:03–01:29:48.)

## Physical locations, exceptions, and records

Machines needing further investigation can be put back into a stack and marked with a yellow sticker. Repaired, unrepaired, and parts equipment are not consistently segregated; William cited limited space. No named stations, fixed aisle scheme, or reliable location taxonomy was confirmed. The pilot Intake can leave a Machine's digital Location unassigned under [ADR 0009](./adr/0009-locationless-intake.md). Future location decisions should reflect actual movement and capacity rather than assume the current floor is already organized that way.

Observed exceptions include bearing noise and uneconomic repairs, non-obvious electrical or mechanical defects, Machines held for further investigation, missing or uncertain parts, card-to-coin conversion needs, cosmetic work found during cleaning, and missing coin-box keys delaying outbound work. When a repair is uneconomic, William may recover usable parts. The precise authorization, disposition, and retest paths for each exception still need ticket-level design. (September 16, 38:05–40:54, 44:15–46:15; September 21, 48:10–56:21, 58:35–59:26, 01:03:48–01:04:47.)

Physical washer and dryer checklists, a cleaning checklist, and a shipping instruction sheet exist as starting materials. The walkthrough did not demonstrate that every item is completed on paper for every Machine. Videos are currently found by searching for a photographed serial/nameplate on a phone. William described proposed QR scanning, attributable work records, reviewed instructions, and a formal release path; these are product requirements and design inputs, not claims about today's recordkeeping. (September 16, 35:05–39:15; September 21, 37:38–40:09, 58:02–58:22, 01:02:48–01:03:28.)

## Confirmed rules and deferred design details

- Keep each physical Machine's identity and history tied to its Load, evidence, testing, repair, cleaning, and shipment; do not confuse listing or sale with production readiness. [ADR 0003](./adr/0003-separate-operational-state-axes.md) keeps the state axes separate.
- Prioritize sold or reserved equipment needing production work. Preliminary acceptance can permit listing before refurbishment, but shipment requires explicit QA Release under the architecture.
- Preserve original test failures, repair and parts evidence, retests, and an attributable QA decision. A clean finish or working video alone is not a QA Release.
- Treat the bearing check as an early disposition signal with owner judgment, not an automatic rule for every washer or dryer.

Production tickets must still determine the exact washer/dryer checklist steps and evidence, which existing instructions count as audited, queue ordering and overrides, repair versus donor-parts approval, parts condition and availability tracking, retest requirements, cosmetic acceptance, QA reviewer and release criteria, physical location model, and packing handoff. The transcripts do not settle these details.
